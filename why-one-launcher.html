Why this exists

A couch PC should feel like a console.

Couchfront is a direct alternative to Playnite and Steam Big Picture. It does less than Playnite today, but it is built around the parts I care about: a controller-first library, fast launching, speed controls, and a process killer. I started with what I wanted and will add more if enough people ask for it.

How it started

I started by adding non-Steam games to Steam. That worked for a while, but controller support, killing a stuck game, resuming after a break, and a dozen smaller things kept getting in the way. Fixing those one at a time turned into a separate launcher.

What I want is simple: a PC that sets up and behaves like a console, while keeping the prices and flexibility of PC games from stores beyond Steam. Couchfront is my attempt at that.

Direct alternative

Steam Big Picture still works well for a Steam-only library. Playnite is the closer comparison: it has plugins, themes, metadata, and emulator support, but it takes a lot of configuration, it does not stop Steam from stealing focus, and it has no global universal controller support — controller behaviour varies per game and per pad, and it cannot turn the pad into a mouse for the rest of Windows.

Couchfront is the smaller option: installed games from several stores, one controller-first library, optional Steam sync, speed controls, a process killer, a download queue for sources you add, and Google Drive save sync. I started with what I use. If you need those Playnite features today, Playnite is the better fit. If you want something focused, try this.

What it does

It finds games installed across Steam, Epic, GOG, Xbox, EA, Ubisoft, Amazon, Battle.net, itch.io, and user configurable custom folders, with smart defaults for each drive at X:\Games. You browse and launch them from one controller-first library. If you still want Steam Big Picture, you can sync the same games into Steam as shortcuts with artwork.

It also adds some things consoles do not have: speed controls for single-player games, a process killer for stuck games, an on-screen keyboard (a physical keyboard still works while it is open), a mouse mode that turns the pad into a system-wide cursor (hold Select + B), controller emulation that makes most pads look like an Xbox 360 controller, a Deals tab that searches live prices across every store it tracks, a Downloads tab that downloads and silently installs from catalogues you add, and optional Google Drive save sync with local snapshots you can restore.

Current cons

  • No emulator platform management. Playnite supports emulators through plugins.
  • No custom plugins. Playnite has a plugin ecosystem; Couchfront ships with built-in themes.
  • Less metadata than Playnite. Artwork comes from SteamGridDB; there is no rich metadata library yet.
  • No storefront. Downloads work from catalogues and links you configure; there is nowhere to buy a game.
  • Windows only. No macOS, Linux, Steam Deck, or Android.

Some of these may get support later if enough people care. For now, I am starting with the features I like.

The short version

A smaller, direct alternative to Playnite and Steam Big Picture. It does less today, but it is built around the couch features I wanted.
